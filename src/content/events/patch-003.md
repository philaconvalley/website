Philly, projection mapping is how a building becomes a screen. At PATCH 003, we brought it down to
the size of a table.

We split the room into five stations. Each station had a projector aimed at the wall and a small
group building on their own laptops. People took turns sending their work to the projector, and
everyone watched each other's ideas land on the same wall.

Everyone built on [projectionMapper](https://github.com/philaconvalley/projectionMapper), our
open-source p5.js template. You drag four corners to fit your art to a surface, and you write the
art in one file, `art.js`. It runs from a downloaded folder, with no install, no account, and no
Wi-Fi. One of our volunteers is not a coder. She downloaded the folder, edited `art.js` in a plain
text editor, and had it running.

Every person left with a project folder they changed themselves and can open again without help.
That is the bar for a PATCH Lab.

Waskar Paulino taught the Lab. Diego Mendoza-Ramos ran the room and the gear. Fatima Conteh and Jay
Turner helped at the door and the stations. Photos by Chris Sanchez.
